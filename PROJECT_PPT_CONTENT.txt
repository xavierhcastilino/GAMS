                                                          SLIDE 2: PROBLEM STATEMENT & MOTIVATION
                                                          --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                                          • The Flaws of Current Attendance Systems:
                                                            - Manual Roll Call: Wastes 10–15 minutes of lecture time; prone to human recording errors.
                                                            - Static QR Codes: Easily photographed and forwarded to absent friends over WhatsApp / Telegram.
                                                            - Biometric Kiosks / RFID: Creates severe hallway bottlenecks; RFID cards are easily handed over ("buddy punching").
                                                            - GPS / Geofencing Apps: Highly vulnerable to fake GPS mock apps; lacks vertical accuracy (e.g., student on 3rd floor marked present for 1st floor).
                                                            - Bluetooth (BLE) Beacons: Radio signals bleed through concrete walls, allowing attendance from outside the room.
                                                          • Core Objective:
                                                            - Create a zero-trust, two-factor proof system verifying: (1) physical presence inside the classroom room, and (2) verified student identity.

                                                          [Visual Suggestion: Infographic comparing proxy vulnerability of QR, GPS, BLE vs. Classroom walls]
                                                          [Speaker Note: "Traditional systems fail because digital tokens easily escape the physical classroom. QR codes and GPS coordinates can be shared instantly. We needed a medium that is physically confined by walls."]


                                                          SLIDE 3: PROPOSED SOLUTION - THE DUAL-PROOF ARCHITECTURE
                                                          --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                                          • Proof 1: Acoustic Physical Proximity (Near-Ultrasound)
                                                            - Teacher’s phone broadcasts an inaudible near-ultrasonic acoustic beacon (18.0 kHz to 19.5 kHz).
                                                            - High-frequency sound cannot penetrate closed classroom walls or windows.
                                                            - Student app records audio and decodes a rotating HMAC cryptographic token via the Goertzel algorithm.
                                                          • Proof 2: Biometric Identity Proof (Live Deep Neural Face Match)
                                                            - Successful token decoding unlocks the strict hardware camera gate.
                                                            - On-device ML Kit verifies a centered, single live human face in real-time.
                                                            - Server-side Python deep learning microservice compares live selfie embedding against enrolled ID photo.
                                                          • Result:
                                                            - Proxies become mathematically and physically impossible.

                                                          [Visual Suggestion: 2-step verification flowchart: (Acoustic Beacon -> Token Decoded) => (Camera Gate Unlocks -> Live Face Match -> Present)]
                                                          [Speaker Note: "GAMS uses a dual-proof protocol: ultrasound proves you are physically inside the four walls of the classroom, and deep learning facial embeddings prove you are who you claim to be."]

                                                          SLIDE 5: DEEP DIVE 1 - ULTRASONIC PROXIMITY PROTOCOL
                                                          --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                                          • Acoustic Frequency Specifications:
                                                            - Frequency Range: 18.0 kHz – 19.5 kHz (inaudible to human ears, within standard 48 kHz mobile sampling rate).
                                                            - Modulation: Frequency-Shift Keying (FSK).
                                                              * 18,000 Hz: Synchronization Preamble (50 ms pulse)
                                                              * 18,500 Hz: Binary Bit '0' (25 ms pulse)
                                                              * 19,000 Hz: Binary Bit '1' (25 ms pulse)
                                                              * 19,500 Hz: Stop / Frame Termination (25 ms pulse)
                                                          • Signal Processing (Receiver):
                                                            - Uses Goertzel Algorithm (Discrete Fourier Transform filter) to compute energy at target frequencies with O(N) efficiency.
                                                          • Cryptographic Security:
                                                            - Token = Truncate8(HMAC-SHA256(SessionSecret, SessionID : WindowIndex)).
                                                            - Tokens rotate automatically every 20 seconds.
                                                            - Server accepts current window + adjacent (-1, +1) windows to accommodate minor clock drift.

                                                          [Visual Suggestion: Frequency spectrogram illustration showing preamble pulse, bit bursts, and pause interval]
                                                          [Speaker Note: "By using the Goertzel algorithm at 18-19.5 kHz, we detect specific target frequencies with minimal mobile CPU usage. The HMAC token rotates every 20 seconds, preventing any audio recording replay attacks."]


                                                          SLIDE 6: DEEP DIVE 2 - HARDWARE GATE & ANTI-PROXY CONTROLS
                                                          --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                                          • Hardware Keystore Device Binding:
                                                            - Accounts are cryptographically bound to a single physical device on first login.
                                                            - Prevents one student from logging into multiple phones to mark proxies for absent peers.
                                                            - Device resets strictly restricted to Admin/HOD authorization.
                                                          • Strict Hardware Camera Gate:
                                                            - Camera is locked until a valid ultrasonic token is decoded by the microphone.
                                                            - No Gallery Access / No File Picker: The app only streams live CameraX sensor frames.
                                                            - Impossible to inject pre-saved photos, digital screen snapshots, or pre-recorded video loops.
                                                          • Ephemeral Data Lifecycle:
                                                            - Audio buffer is processed in RAM and immediately flushed.
                                                            - Captured selfies are destroyed from mobile memory immediately after submission.

                                                          [Visual Suggestion: Flow diagram highlighting security check gates: Device Check -> Audio Gate -> Camera Gate -> Submission]
                                                          [Speaker Note: "We ensure zero bypass: the camera hardware stays completely locked until the acoustic token is decoded. Furthermore, device binding prevents one student from carrying a friend's credentials."]


                                                          SLIDE 7: DEEP DIVE 3 - BIOMETRIC FACE RECOGNITION PIPELINE
                                                          --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                                          • Stage 1: On-Device Framing (Google ML Kit)
                                                            - Real-time client-side analysis ensures exactly 1 face is detected.
                                                            - Validates that the face is centered within the inner 70% viewport before allowing capture.
                                                          • Stage 2: Deep Face Detection (YuNet ONNX)
                                                            - High-speed neural detector (`face_detection_yunet_2023mar.onnx`).
                                                            - Accurately detects facial landmarks (eyes, nose, mouth) even under tilted angles.
                                                          • Stage 3: Embedding Extraction (SFace ONNX)
                                                            - Deep face recognition model (`face_recognition_sface_2021dec.onnx`).
                                                            - Generates an L2-normalized 128-dimensional mathematical vector.
                                                          • Stage 4: Cosine Similarity Matching:
                                                            - Cosine Similarity = (u · v) / (||u|| * ||v||)
                                                            - Score >= 0.60: Automatically marked PRESENT.
                                                            - Score < 0.60: Routed to Teacher Review Queue (never auto-failed).

                                                          [Visual Suggestion: Step-by-step image processing graphic showing face bounding box -> aligned face crop -> 128-d vector bar -> similarity score]
                                                          [Speaker Note: "We combine edge ML Kit detection for instantaneous user guidance with server-side YuNet and SFace models for 128-dimensional embedding extraction, computing cosine similarity in milliseconds."]

                                                          SLIDE 9: HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP REVIEW QUEUE & FALLBACK
                                                          --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                                          • The Problem with Pure Automation:
                                                            - Poor classroom lighting, new spectacles, or facial hair changes can cause marginal score drops.
                                                          • The GAMS Solution (Zero Auto-Rejection):
                                                            - High confidence (Score >= 0.60) -> Auto-approved as 'present'.
                                                            - Marginal/Low confidence (Score < 0.60) -> Sent to Teacher's Review Queue.
                                                          • Side-by-Side Verification:
                                                            - Teacher sees student's official ID reference photo alongside the live captured selfie.
                                                            - One-tap 'Approve' or 'Reject' with full audit trail.
                                                          • Hardware Fallback (Manual Override):
                                                            - If a student phone has a damaged microphone, teacher can manually mark them from the post-session absent list with a mandatory logged reason.

                                                          [Visual Suggestion: Screenshot of Teacher's Review Queue with side-by-side enrolled ID photo vs. live selfie with Green/Red action buttons]
                                                          [Speaker Note: "To protect students from false rejections due to bad lighting, GAMS employs a Human-in-the-Loop review queue. Borderline matches are sent to the teacher for one-tap side-by-side verification."]

                                                          SLIDE 11: PRIVACY & COMPLIANCE (DPDP ACT 2023)
                                                          --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                                          • Built to comply with India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023:
                                                            - Purpose Limitation: Audio data is processed strictly for token frequency detection and never recorded to disk.
                                                            - Ephemeral Storage: Live selfies are deleted from both client and server storage immediately after matching / session closure.
                                                            - Non-Reversible Biometrics: Only 128-dimensional floating-point vectors are stored. Raw faces cannot be reconstructed from vectors.
                                                            - No Continuous Tracking: App accesses mic and camera only during the active 30-second verification window; no background location tracking.

                                                          [Visual Suggestion: Compliance badge graphic showing DPDP Act 2023, Privacy by Design, and Ephemeral Biometrics icons]
                                                          [Speaker Note: "Privacy is designed into GAMS from day one. In strict accordance with the DPDP Act 2023, raw audio is never recorded, selfies are temporary and deleted after review, and only one-way mathematical embeddings are preserved."]

                                                          SLIDE 14: ENGINEERING CHALLENGES OVERCOME
                                                          --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                                          • 1. Android Noise Suppression Algorithms:
                                                            - Problem: OS voice-enhancement filters treat 18+ kHz frequencies as unwanted noise and mute them.
                                                            - Solution: Configured AudioRecord with `MediaRecorder.AudioSource.UNPROCESSED` with automatic fallback to `DEFAULT`.
                                                          • 2. Clock Drift Across Devices:
                                                            - Problem: Teacher and student device clocks can be out of sync by several seconds.
                                                            - Solution: Server validates tokens across a sliding window of [Current, Current - 1, Current + 1] windows.
                                                          • 3. Extreme Classroom Lighting & Angles:
                                                            - Problem: Dark corners and extreme tilt angles resulted in failed face crops.
                                                            - Solution: Embedded client-side Google ML Kit guidance box that only triggers capture once the face is centered and lit.

                                                                      TOP 10 VIVA / PROJECT DEFENSE QUESTIONS & ANSWERS
                                                          ================================================================================
                                                          Q1: Why choose ultrasound instead of Bluetooth (BLE)?
                                                          A1: BLE signals travel through walls, floors, and doors (up to 30-50m), allowing students in the hallway or canteen to mark attendance. Ultrasound (18-19.5 kHz) is physically blocked by walls, ensuring strict physical presence inside the room.

                                                          Q2: Can a student record the ultrasonic tone and send it to an absent friend via WhatsApp?
                                                          A2: No. First, the token rotates every 20 seconds using HMAC-SHA256, so the recorded audio expires almost immediately. Second, messaging apps like WhatsApp compress audio with low-pass filters that cut off all frequencies above 16 kHz, destroying the data.

                                                          Q3: Can humans hear this sound? Is it harmful to health?
                                                          A3: No. 18 to 19.5 kHz is above the average adult human hearing threshold (presbycusis). The bursts are played at standard media volume for short intervals, fully complying with WHO and OSHA acoustic safety guidelines.

                                                          Q4: What if a student's phone has a broken microphone?
                                                          A4: GAMS features a human-in-the-loop fallback. If the phone fails to decode after 30 seconds, the teacher can manually mark the student present from the post-session absent list with an audited reason.

                                                          Q5: Why did you use YuNet + SFace instead of FaceNet or dlib?
                                                          A5: YuNet and SFace are state-of-the-art ONNX models included in modern OpenCV DNN, specifically optimized for edge/CPU inference. YuNet executes face detection in ~25 ms, and SFace produces compact 128-d vectors in ~60 ms without requiring heavy GPU hardware.

                                                          Q6: How do you prevent a student from taking a photo of a photo?
                                                          A6: The CameraX integration locks out the Android gallery/file picker completely. Furthermore, client-side ML Kit evaluates face geometry, and the architecture supports passive liveness detection (MiniFASNet) to analyze screen moiré and skin reflections.

                                                          Q7: What stops a student from logging into their friend's account on their phone?
                                                          A7: Hardware device binding. On first login, the student's account is cryptographically bound to the device's hardware Keystore. Attendance requests from an unbound device are rejected by the server.

                                                          Q8: How does GAMS comply with the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023?
                                                          A8: By practicing data minimization: raw audio is never recorded, live selfies are deleted immediately after session verification, and only non-reversible mathematical 128-d vector embeddings are stored in the database.

                                                          Q9: How do you handle clock discrepancies between phones and the server?
                                                          A9: The server verifies tokens against a sliding time window (Current window, Window - 1, and Window + 1), providing a 20-second tolerance window for network latency and clock drift.

                                                          Q10: Why use Node.js and Python together rather than just one language?
                                                          A10: Separation of concerns. Node.js handles fast, asynchronous I/O and REST client requests for hundreds of simultaneous students, while Python handles vector math and neural network inference in an isolated, independently scalable microservice.
